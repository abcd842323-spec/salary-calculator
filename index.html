Where your CTC goes

    How your in-hand salary is calculated

    CTC is not your salary. Cost to Company includes money you never see monthly — mainly your employer's PF contribution and gratuity. The calculation goes in four steps:

    1. Split the CTC. Basic (default 40% of CTC) → HRA (default 50% of basic) → employer PF and gratuity come out → whatever remains is special allowance, plus any bonus you entered.
    2. Gross monthly salary = basic + HRA + special allowance. Employer PF and gratuity sit inside CTC but are not paid to you every month.
    3. Deductions. Your own PF (12% of basic, or ₹1,800/month if capped at the ₹15,000 wage ceiling), professional tax (state-wise), and income tax (TDS) are subtracted.
    4. In-hand = gross − PF − professional tax − TDS. Bonus, when paid, is taxed at your slab and lands separately.

    Worked example (the default numbers): ₹12,00,000 CTC → basic ₹4,80,000, HRA ₹2,40,000, employer PF ₹21,600 (capped), gratuity ₹23,088 → special allowance ₹4,35,312. Gross salary is ₹11,55,312 a year. Under the new regime, taxable income after the ₹75,000 standard deduction is ₹10,80,312; the slab tax of about ₹48,000 is fully wiped out by the Section 87A rebate, so income tax is ₹0. After PF (₹21,600) and Maharashtra professional tax (₹2,500), in-hand comes to about ₹94,268 a month — roughly 94% of CTC reaches you as cash.

    Tax rules used (FY 2025-26)

    New regime (default): no tax up to ₹4 lakh; 5% from ₹4–8 lakh; 10% from ₹8–12 lakh; 15% from ₹12–16 lakh; 20% from ₹16–20 lakh; 25% from ₹20–24 lakh; 30% above ₹24 lakh. Salaried employees get a ₹75,000 standard deduction and a Section 87A rebate up to ₹60,000 — together that means zero tax on salary up to ₹12 lakh. HRA exemption and 80C are not available.

    Old regime: no tax up to ₹2.5 lakh; 5% to ₹5 lakh; 20% to ₹10 lakh; 30% above. Standard deduction ₹50,000, rebate up to ₹12,500 (Section 87A, taxable income up to ₹5 lakh), plus HRA exemption and deductions like 80C (up to ₹1.5 lakh) and 80D.

    HRA exemption (old regime) is the lowest of: actual HRA received; rent paid minus 10% of basic; and 50% of basic in a metro city (40% elsewhere).

    A 4% health & education cess is added to the tax in both regimes. Surcharge applies above ₹50 lakh taxable income (capped at 25% under the new regime). Slabs must be re-checked after every Union Budget.

    Common questions

    Why is my in-hand so much lower than my CTC?

    Because CTC bundles employer PF and gratuity (paid later, not monthly), and your own PF, professional tax and TDS come out of the gross. On a ₹12 lakh CTC under the new regime, about ₹11.3 lakh reaches you over the year — the gap is mostly the two PF contributions and gratuity, which are still your money, just not monthly cash.

    Is employer PF really part of my money?

    Yes — it goes into your EPF account along with your own contribution and earns interest (8.25% for FY 2024-25 as per the research notes). It is your retirement money, just not monthly cash. Gratuity is paid only when you leave after 5 years of service.

    Which regime should I choose?

    For most salaried people without a home loan or large deductions, the new regime wins because of the ₹12 lakh zero-tax window. The old regime can win if your HRA exemption plus 80C and other deductions are large — the comparison above uses your own numbers, so check both boxes before you file.

    Why does professional tax change by state?

    Professional tax is levied by state governments, each with its own slabs: Maharashtra charges about ₹2,500 a year, Karnataka about ₹2,400, while Delhi, Uttar Pradesh and Haryana charge none. Your payslip follows the state you work in, not where you live.

    Does this include ESI, labour welfare fund or other payslip items?

    No. ESI applies only when monthly wages are ₹21,000 or less, and small items like labour welfare fund (₹25–50 in some states) are left out. Companies also structure CTC differently — compare the components, not just the total, when you check an offer.

    Reading an offer letter

    • Compare in-hand, not CTC. A higher CTC with a big variable-pay or gratuity share can pay less monthly than a lower, fixed-heavy CTC.
    • Check the basic %. Higher basic means higher PF and gratuity — less cash now, more retirement savings. Lower basic does the opposite.
    • Ask if PF is capped. "PF on ₹15,000 ceiling" puts roughly ₹1,800 less into EPF each month than PF on full basic — and puts that cash in your hand instead.
    • Treat bonus honestly. Variable pay is taxed at your slab and may not be paid in full; this calculator keeps it out of the monthly figure on purpose.
